Planet Transits

Spotting transiting planets from lightcurves can be tricky...

SHORT PERIOD PLANET/MANY TRANSIT

Some transits are have a characteristic U-shape. It is always good to look out for this!

Some like to hide in the stellar variability and may be more difficult to find.

The smaller the gap between subsequent transits, the quicker the planet completes a full orbit around the host star.

LONG PERIOD PLANET/SINGLE TRANSIT

If the orbital period is very long, there may only be one transit visible!

MULTI-PLANET SYSTEMS

Uneven spacing is indicative of there being multiple planets.

Eclipsing Binaries

When one star passes in front of the other we see a dip in the lightcurve, known as an eclipsing binary. These tend to be more V-shaped.

ALTERNATING DIPS

Lightcurves of eclipsing binaries can often be identified due to the repetition two dips of different depths.

Stellar Variability

The natural variability of stars can have a strong effect on the observed lightcurves, making the transits harder to spot.

STARSPOTS

As stars rotate, these spots move around with them, resulting in variable lightcurves.

PULSATING

Certain stars periodically increase and decrease in brightness. These variations can be very rapid, and can make transiting planets very difficult to see.

Systematic Effects
